# Field Report 00 Plan

This report defines how field reports should be written before any data are collected.

## Governing question

How will the project report fieldwork without overstating what the data can show?

## Purpose of field reports

Field reports are the project's audit trail.

They should document:

- what was attempted
- what was captured
- what failed
- what changed
- what the data can and cannot support

They are not promotional updates.
They are not proof documents.
They are not participant stories.

## Report sequence

Planned reports:

- `field-report-00-plan.md`: reporting rules and expectations
- `field-report-01-friend-pre-pilot.md`: template and later report for the first friend pre-pilot
- later reports only if the pipeline survives the pre-pilot

## Required sections

Each completed field report should include:

- scope
- participants and conditions, using codes only
- capture setup
- processing results
- usable and excluded data
- blind observation comparison
- feature extraction results
- left-right comparison
- clustering attempt, if any
- what broke
- what changed before the next round
- strongest honest claim

## Evidence standard

Every field-report claim should name its evidence.

Use:

> Three of four normal shod trials produced usable hip, knee, ankle, and pelvis curves.

Do not use:

> The method works.

Use:

> The blind memo proposed delayed left stabilization, but the left-right stance curves did not show a consistent timing difference.

Do not use:

> The observer was wrong.

## Participant privacy

Reports must follow [Data Handling](../docs/ethics/data-handling.md) and [Public Sharing Rules](../docs/ethics/public-sharing-rules.md).

Use participant codes only.
Do not include raw video stills.
Do not include identifiable anecdotes.
Do not include injury details unless necessary and consented, and even then keep them general.

## Failure reporting

A field report should make failure visible.

Useful failure categories:

- capture failure
- consent or comfort issue
- processing failure
- segmentation failure
- feature failure
- observer mismatch
- confound
- unclear concept
- analysis too small

Do not convert every failure into a new theory.

## Strongest allowed conclusion

The strongest conclusion after the friend pre-pilot will likely be methodological:

> Under these recording conditions, this pipeline did or did not produce interpretable gait-cycle traces for the selected features.

That is enough for the first field report.
