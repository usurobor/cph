# Walking Is Not a Style

Walking is a recurring whole-body load-transfer strategy, not a visual aesthetic.

## The distinction

When we watch someone walk, we notice rhythm, posture, arm swing, foot placement, and overall impression. These observations are real, but they mix the movement with the observer. Camera angle, clothing, mood, familiarity, body size, and cultural expectation all enter the observation before the biomechanics do.

A style is how walking looks.
A strategy is how walking works.

This project is about strategy.

## What a walking step actually is

After the foot contacts the ground, load enters the body. What happens next is not arbitrary:

- Some regions stiffen to transmit force.
- Some regions yield to absorb it.
- Some regions rotate to redirect it.
- Some regions stabilize to prevent collapse.
- Some regions accelerate to prepare the next step.

This organization happens in time. It has sequence, duration, coupling, and coordination. It can be measured.

## Why this matters for the project

If walking is treated as a style, the research becomes classification of appearances. The instrument becomes the camera. The ground truth becomes the observer's label. The AI learns to reproduce human impression.

If walking is treated as a strategy, the research becomes detection of recurring coordination patterns in biomechanical time series. The instrument becomes OpenCap. The ground truth becomes the data. The AI learns to find structure that may or may not match what the observer expected.

The second path is harder. It is also the only path that can surprise us.

## The safe sentence

> Under this condition, this recording shows this movement pattern.

Not:

> This person walks like this.
