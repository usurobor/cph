# Load Transfer Strategy

A load-transfer strategy is the observable organization by which a walking body accepts, redirects, stabilizes, and returns load during a gait cycle.

It is a functional description, not a personality description.

## Governing question

How does this recorded step move load through the body after ground contact?

The question is deliberately local:

- this recorded step
- this condition
- this side
- this participant code
- this trial

It does not ask what kind of person the participant is.

## What "load transfer" means here

During walking, body weight and ground reaction enter the system through the stance foot. The body then has to solve several linked problems:

- accept load without collapse
- keep the center of mass moving
- coordinate pelvis, trunk, and limbs
- control rotation
- prepare the opposite limb for swing and contact
- return or redirect energy for the next step

A strategy is the repeatable way these problems are solved in time.

## What may vary

Two steps can have the same general task and still use different strategies. Differences may appear in:

- where motion is absorbed
- where motion is blocked
- how quickly the body stabilizes after contact
- whether rotation is distributed or concentrated
- whether propulsion appears early, late, smooth, or interrupted
- whether one side shows a different sequence than the other
- whether the pattern changes under speed, footwear, fatigue, or attention

These differences are not automatically better or worse. They are descriptions to be tested.

## Relation to support paths

A support path is the project's name for a candidate recurring load-transfer organization. The broader concept is the strategy. The narrower research object is the inferred support path within gait-cycle data.

That distinction matters because the project may find load-transfer differences that do not resolve into stable support-path families. That would be a useful result.

## What this project does not infer

The project does not infer:

- diagnosis
- injury risk for a participant
- psychological type
- athletic potential
- moral or aesthetic value
- permanent identity

The project may say:

> Under this condition, this recording shows delayed right-side stabilization after contact.

It may not say:

> This participant is a delayed-stabilization type.

## Practical use in analysis

The phrase "load-transfer strategy" should be used when describing a functional hypothesis. For example:

- "This trial may show a pelvis-led load-transfer strategy."
- "The left stance cycles show a later push-off timing than the right stance cycles."
- "The qualitative memo proposed trunk-dominant control, but the curves do not support that interpretation."

Every such statement should be traceable to the gait-cycle unit and to measurable features.
